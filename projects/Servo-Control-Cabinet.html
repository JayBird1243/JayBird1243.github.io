Build cabinet

  1. Read and plan. Worked from the electrical drawings to lay out the backplane: servo drive, transformer, 24 VDC supply, I/O, terminals and duct.
  2. Wire. The 240 VAC input feeds the servo drive directly and a transformer that steps it down to 120 VAC. The 120 VAC runs a 24 VDC power supply, which powers the I/O. Motor and signal wiring, with separation between power and low-level signal runs.
  3. Label everything. Every conductor and terminal labelled to the drawing for easy troubleshooting.
  4. Route with Panduit. Clean duct routing so the cabinet can be serviced and extended without pulling it apart.

Commissioning machine

  • Installation of the finished panel in its designated machine.
  • Power-up sequence and I/O verification against the drawings before any motion.
  • PID calibration on the servo alongside the integrator: response, overshoot and settling, adjusted until the motion profile matched the requirement.

What I took from it practice

My first large scale professional project. I learned extensive tooling and electrical practices that helped me throughout the rest of my co-op.
