Servo Control Cabinet

A servo control cabinet rebuilt from the electrical drawings, wired and labelled so a technician can troubleshoot it without me. I then installed it in its machine and helped the integrators tune the servo.

Scope
Full cabinet rebuild from drawings
Power
240 VAC in, stepped down to 120 VAC and 24 VDC
Commissioning
Installation, power-up and I/O checks
Tuning
Servo PID calibration with the integrators
  • I built the panel from the electrical drawings: backplane layout, power and signal wiring, terminations and labelling.
  • I installed it in its machine and ran power-up and I/O checks before handing it to production.
  • I helped the integrators calibrate the servo PID parameters.
01

What I built

  1. Laid out the backplane from the electrical drawings: servo drive, transformer, 24 VDC supply, I/O, terminals and duct.
  2. Wired the power side. The 240 VAC input feeds the servo drive directly and a transformer that steps it down to 120 VAC. The 120 VAC runs a 24 VDC power supply, which powers the I/O.
  3. Wired the motor and signal runs, with power kept apart from low-level signals.
  4. Labelled every conductor and terminal to the drawing.
  5. Routed everything in Panduit duct, so the cabinet can be serviced without pulling it apart.
02

Commissioning

  • Installed the finished panel in its machine.
  • Ran power-up and I/O checks against the drawings before any motion.
  • Calibrated the servo PID with the integrators, adjusting response, overshoot and settling until the motion matched the requirement.
03

What I took from it

This was my first large professional project. I learned tooling and electrical practices that I used for the rest of my co-op.
