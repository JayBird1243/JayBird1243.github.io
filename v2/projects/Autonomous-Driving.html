Autonomous Driving

A semester team project: a small robot taxi on a Raspberry Pi that drives a miniature city, picks up fares and follows traffic rules. I wrote the navigation and fare logic and built the brake and turn lights.

Team
4 people
Timeline
~12 weeks
Platform
Raspberry Pi 4B, PiCar-X
My part
Navigation, fare logic, lights
  • I built where the car went and why. Route planning with Dijkstra, position tracking between GPS fixes, and a fare picker that ranks rides by profit per minute.
  • Position data was slow. GPS fixes arrived up to 4 seconds late, so I estimated position from speed and heading in between.
  • Parts worked, integration did not finish. Pathfinding and fare logic ran correctly. Steering drift and vision under motion were still open when the competition was cancelled.
01

The system

Three layers ran in one loop on the Pi. Sensors fed decisions, decisions sent commands to the motors and lights. My parts are in amber.

02

What I built

Pathfinding

A modified Dijkstra in NetworkX. Each road's weight is its length plus penalties. Any road where the car changes direction counts double, and roads in risk zones are made to look longer. The planner then prefers straight, safe routes even when they are a bit longer.

Position between GPS fixes

The course GPS (VPFS) could be up to 4 seconds behind. I kept an internal x/y position and advanced it from the car's speed and heading between fixes. Pathfinding always used the latest combined estimate.

x += v · cos θ · Δt
y += v · sin θ · Δt

Fare selection

The car polled the course server for open rides. For each one I estimated the total time: driving to the pickup, the ride itself, and a penalty for turns. Fare divided by that time gives profit per minute, and the car claimed the highest score. The nearest ride is often not the best one.

Brake and turn lights

I hand-soldered the light board on a 15 × 8 cm protoboard: red and yellow LEDs, each with a 100 Ω resistor sized for the Pi's 3.3 V GPIO. The code drove them alongside the navigation logic, so the car signalled its turns and stops.

The rest of the system

Built by my teammates.

Teammate

Lane detection

OpenCV on live camera frames, using a Hough line transform to find white and dashed yellow lane lines. Reliable on straights, harder on curves.

Teammate

Object detection

A retrained EfficientDet-Lite model on a Coral Edge TPU, trained on road signs and rubber ducks. Good when still, weaker with motion blur.

Teammate

Motion control

A PD controller turned lane error into steering. Speed dropped in steps as the steering angle grew.

More detail

Lane detection

Each frame went through grayscale, Gaussian blur, binary threshold and Canny edge detection, then a trapezoid road mask before the Hough transform. Each yellow dash returned four lines (its edges), so a horizontal-line filter dropped stop-line noise and an outlier filter merged what was left into one line per side. Curves were hard because the Hough transform looks for straight lines.

Object detection

Training images came from Google Street View shots of Ontario roads plus rubber ducks, labelled with bounding boxes in MakeSense.ai and exported as Pascal VOC. If confidence on a class stayed under 0.67, the team added data and retrained. Running on the TPU kept inference off the Pi's CPU.

Motion control

Gains were kp = 0.4 and kd = 0.15. Speed was 100% under 5° of steering, 80% at 5–10°, 60% at 10–15° and 40% above 15°. If the lane was lost for 10 frames or fewer, the car held its last steering at 50% speed with 0.7× damping. Past 10 frames it straightened the wheels and crept forward at 30%.

Architecture

One central loop called each module on its own schedule: lane following every frame, GPS several times a second, fare checks less often. Each subsystem had a simple interface so the team could integrate separately built parts.

04

Outcome

Worked

  • Pathfinding and fare selection ran correctly.
  • Lane following held for about 7 seconds on straight roads before drifting.
  • Object detection reached 0.67+ confidence in controlled tests.
  • The car course-corrected using the grayscale sensors.

Did not

  • The steering servo pulled right even with a neutral command, a mechanical calibration problem.
  • Vision got worse while moving: motion blur and changing light, and track noise once off the simulator.
  • The competition was cancelled before we fixed these.
